# 4. Voiceover and Editing

## Voiceover options

- **Your own voice** — best for trust and originality, and free. Record in
  a quiet room with your phone and a free app like Audacity to remove noise.
- **Text-to-speech (TTS)** — faster and fully faceless. Quality varies;
  listen to the whole track before using it. Avoid robotic, monotone voices
  that make viewers click away.

Whichever you choose, the pacing matters more than the voice: steady speed,
clear pauses between sections, and no background music louder than the voice.

## Visuals

Faceless videos usually use one or more of:

- Free stock footage and photos (Pexels, Pixabay — check the licence)
- Simple maps, timelines, and diagrams you make yourself
- AI-generated images for scenes that have no real footage

Rule of thumb: change the visual every 3–6 seconds. A single static image
for a whole video loses viewers fast.

## Editing workflow

1. Lay the voiceover on the timeline first.
2. Cut visuals to match what the voice is saying, section by section.
3. Add captions/subtitles — many viewers watch with sound off.
4. Add quiet background music (YouTube Audio Library is free and safe).
5. Watch the whole video once before exporting. Fix anything that feels
   slow or confusing.

## Free editing tools

- **CapCut (free tier):** fast, good captions, beginner-friendly.
- **DaVinci Resolve (free):** professional, steeper learning curve.
- **Audacity (free):** clean up voice recordings.

## Quality checklist before upload

- [ ] Audio is clear, no buzzing or echo
- [ ] No copyrighted music or clips you do not have rights to
- [ ] Facts in the script were checked
- [ ] Thumbnail matches the video (no clickbait that the video does not deliver)
- [ ] Title and description are written (see guide 05)
