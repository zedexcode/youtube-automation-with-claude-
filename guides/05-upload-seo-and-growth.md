# 5. Upload, SEO, and Growth

## Titles

A good title does two things: it tells the viewer exactly what they get,
and it creates curiosity. Compare:

- Weak: "History video part 5"
- Strong: "Why the Titanic's lookouts had no binoculars"

Keep titles under 60 characters when possible so they are not cut off.

## Descriptions and tags

- First 2 lines of the description matter most — they show in search. Put
  the video's promise and main keywords there.
- Add chapters (timestamps) for videos over 5 minutes.
- Tags help a little; the title, description, and what viewers actually
  watch matter far more.

## Thumbnails

- One clear subject, 3 words maximum, high contrast.
- Make it in Canva (free) at 1280 × 720.
- The thumbnail and title should work together, not repeat each other.

## Publishing rhythm

- Pick a schedule you can keep for 3 months (for example, every Tuesday
  and Friday). Consistency helps both viewers and the algorithm.
- Upload at a time your audience is awake — check YouTube Analytics >
  Audience to see when your viewers are online.

## Reading analytics (after 10 videos)

Look at these three numbers first:

1. **Click-through rate (CTR):** below 2% usually means the title or
   thumbnail needs work.
2. **Average view duration:** if viewers leave in the first 30 seconds,
   the hook needs work.
3. **Traffic source:** if most views come from browse/search, your niche
   and titles are working.

Double down on the videos that performed best, and make follow-ups on the
same topics.

## Monetisation, realistically

The YouTube Partner Program currently requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000
valid public watch hours (requirements change — check YouTube's official
page). Reaching that usually takes months of steady uploads. Treat early
months as practice, not income.
