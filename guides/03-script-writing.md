# 3. Script Writing with Claude

## The structure of a good video script

Most successful faceless videos follow the same shape:

1. **Hook (first 15–30 seconds):** the most surprising or interesting part
   of the story, stated plainly. No long introductions.
2. **Setup:** the context a new viewer needs, in 2–3 short sentences.
3. **Main story:** told in short sections, each ending with a reason to
   keep watching ("but there was one problem...").
4. **Payoff:** the conclusion or twist the hook promised.
5. **Call to action:** one sentence — subscribe, or watch the next video.

## Writing with Claude, step by step

1. **Outline first.** Ask Claude for an outline, not a full script. Check
   the outline for accuracy and flow before going further.
2. **Draft section by section.** Generate one section at a time so you can
   fix problems early.
3. **Edit for spoken language.** Read the script out loud. If a sentence is
   hard to say, rewrite it. Spoken scripts use short sentences and simple
   words.
4. **Fact-check.** Verify dates, names, and numbers from a second source.
   Never publish a fact you only saw in an AI answer.

## Script length guide

| Video length | Words (approx.) |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| 5 minutes    | 650–750         |
| 8 minutes    | 1,000–1,200     |
| 10 minutes   | 1,300–1,500     |

Spoken English runs at roughly 130–150 words per minute.

## Example prompt

> "Write a YouTube script outline for a video titled 'Why the Titanic's
> lookouts had no binoculars'. Audience: general viewers, no history
> background. Structure: hook, setup, 3 main sections, payoff. Keep each
> section to 2 bullet points. Do not invent quotes or statistics."
