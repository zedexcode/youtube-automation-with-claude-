# 6. Case Study: A Real Channel's First Week

This guide is not theory. It is the real first-week numbers from the
author's own channel, **Ember & Oath Stories** — an AI-movie channel
(faceless, AI-generated werewolf romance movies). Use it to calibrate
your expectations: real numbers are messier than the success stories.

> Figures are from YouTube Studio, recorded 11 November 2026, three days
> after the first full movie went public. Your numbers will differ; the
> *lessons* are what transfer.

## The channel

- **Name:** Ember & Oath Stories (@EmberOathStories)
- **Created:** 5 November 2026
- **Niche:** AI-generated werewolf romance movies (full movies + shorts)
- **First full movie:** "The Wolf King Rejected the Healer Who Saved His
  Pack… Then the Oath Bound Him to Keep Her | Full Movie" — 11 minutes 47
  seconds, public Sunday 8 November 2026, 23:00 PKT
- **Production:** script and story consistency with Claude, voiceover with
  ElevenLabs, images with Gemini/Flow, assembly with ffmpeg, music "Dark
  Cinematic" (Pixabay, licence on file)

## Lesson 1: Suggested is where a new channel lives or dies

Three quarters of this movie's views came from **Suggested videos** —
YouTube recommending it next to other videos. Browse (the home page) gave
only 14 views, and search gave 1. For a new channel, almost nobody finds
you by searching; YouTube has to *place* your video next to something
people already watch. That means your title and thumbnail are competing
with the video the viewer is already watching — not with an empty page.

## Lesson 2: A 2.1% CTR is a warning, not a disaster

Guide 05 says below 2% usually means the title or thumbnail needs work.
This movie sits at 2.1% overall — and only 1.9% on Suggested, where most
impressions come from. But look closer: Browse CTR was **7.1%** on 42
impressions. The same thumbnail and title performed very differently in
two placements. That tells you the problem is not simply "bad thumbnail" —
it is the *match* between the video and the audience Suggested shows it
to. A thumbnail can be good and still get a low CTR if it is shown to the
wrong viewers.

## Lesson 3: Thumbnail iteration has diminishing returns

The thumbnail for this movie went through **13 versions**. Three of them
were rated by an independent judge (Claude): v9 scored 9.0/10, v13 scored
8.9, v12 scored 8.8. The judge's advice was to **stop iterating** — the
three best versions were within 0.2 of each other, inside the margin of
error, and the composition was capped near 9. If none of them lifts the
Suggested CTR well past 2%, the limit is the title or the video's match
to its audience, not the thumbnail.

The practical rule: polish until your best versions are close together,
then test the best one live and move on. Endless polishing is the most
common way beginners waste their first month.

## Lesson 4: Keywords are cheap, so use all of them

The channel's keyword field was empty at first. After research (competitor
tags + keyword research tools), 22 keywords were saved — 441 of the 500
characters YouTube allows — mixing high-volume terms ("ai movies",
"ai movie full movie") with niche terms ("werewolf romance", "rejected
mate", "fated mates", "alpha werewolf"). Keyword research is free work
you do once; it costs nothing to fill the field properly.

## Lesson 5: The first 10 videos are the real test

This channel published 1 full movie + 2 shorts in its first week and got
97 views on the movie. That is normal. The plan from guide 01 applies:
publish 10 videos on a fixed schedule, *then* read the analytics and
double down on what worked. One video's numbers cannot tell you whether
the channel works — ten can.

## What to copy from this case study

- A clear niche, stated in one sentence, before the first upload.
- A repeatable production workflow (script → voice → visuals → edit →
  upload) instead of improvising every video.
- Thumbnail work done in a small number of versions, judged honestly,
  then tested live — not polished forever.
- Keywords researched once and filled properly.
- Analytics read after a batch of videos, not after one.

## What not to copy

- Expecting fast growth. 97 views and 0 subscribers in 3 days is the
  normal starting point, not a failure.
- Judging a thumbnail only by its CTR in one placement. Compare
  Suggested, Browse, and Search separately.
- Changing everything at once. If you change the thumbnail, the title,
  and the keywords together, you will never know which change mattered.
