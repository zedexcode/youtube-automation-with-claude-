# 2. Niche and Research

## Why the niche matters more than the tools

YouTube promotes videos to people who already watch similar content. A clear
niche tells the algorithm who your videos are for. Channels that post about
everything usually grow slower than channels about one thing.

## Good beginner niches

- History explainers ("Why did the Roman Empire fall?")
- Geography and maps
- Simple science explainers
- Sports stories and analysis
- Tech explainers for beginners
- Motivational/business stories (crowded — needs a fresh angle)

## How to validate a niche (30-minute check)

1. Search YouTube for your niche. Are there channels with 10k–500k
   subscribers posting regularly? That is a good sign — it means an audience
   exists but the space is not only giants.
2. Check their recent videos. Do smaller channels in the niche get views?
   If only million-subscriber channels get views, the niche may be too hard.
3. Ask: can you make 50 video ideas in this niche without repeating
   yourself? If yes, it has depth. If no, it is too narrow.

## Using Claude for research

Ask Claude to brainstorm sub-niches and angles, then verify everything
yourself. AI can invent facts, especially dates and statistics, so check
important claims against a reliable source before recording.

Example prompt (more in `../prompts/claude-prompts.md`):

> "List 10 underserved sub-niches inside the history niche for a faceless
> YouTube channel, and for each one give 3 video ideas and why it might
> attract viewers."

## Pick one niche and stick to it

Write your niche down in one sentence, for example:

> "A faceless channel explaining world history through single-event stories
> for viewers aged 16–30."

Every video idea should fit that sentence. If it does not, save it for later.
